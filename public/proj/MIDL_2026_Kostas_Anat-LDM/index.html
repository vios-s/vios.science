Anatomy-Grounded Weakly Supervised Prompt Tuning for Chest X-ray Latent Diffusion Models

Konstantinos Vilouras1, Ilias Stogiannidis1, Junyu Yan1, Alison Q. Smithard1,2, Sotirios A. Tsaftaris1
1 School of Engineering, University of Edinburgh 2 Canon Medical Research Europe Ltd.
Paper Code BibTeX

Abstract

Latent Diffusion Models have shown remarkable results in text-guided image synthesis in recent years. In the domain of natural (RGB) images, recent works have shown that such models can be adapted to various vision–language downstream tasks with little to no supervision involved. On the contrary, text-to-image Latent Diffusion Models remain relatively underexplored in the field of medical imaging, primarily due to limited data availability (e.g., due to privacy concerns). In this work, focusing on the chest X-ray modality, we first demonstrate that a standard text-conditioned Latent Diffusion Model has not learned to align clinically relevant information in free-text radiology reports with the corresponding areas of the given scan. Then, to alleviate this issue, we propose a fine-tuning framework to improve multi-modal alignment in a pre-trained model such that it can be efficiently repurposed for downstream tasks such as phrase grounding. Our method sets a new state-of-the-art on a standard benchmark dataset (MS-CXR), while also exhibiting robust performance on out-of-distribution data (VinDr-CXR). We further validate our approach through a pilot qualitative study and an experiment on grounded disease classification.

Keywords: Chest X-rays · Text-to-Image Latent Diffusion Models · Phrase Grounding

Contributions

  • A novel approach to improving image–text alignment in biomedical VLP by extracting a pathology-localisation supervision signal directly from unstructured radiology reports — combining a clinical entity recognition model (RadGraph-XL) with annotations of anatomical regions commonly depicted in CXRs.
  • An efficient, parameter-light fine-tuning framework that steers a pre-trained LDM's cross-attention activations toward the anatomical area specified in text by updating only the anatomy token embeddings.
  • State-of-the-art phrase grounding on the MS-CXR benchmark and on out-of-distribution VinDr-CXR data, further supported by a pilot radiologist study and a grounded disease classification experiment.

Method

We derive a weak 2D supervision signal from free-text reports and use it to steer cross-attention activations. RadGraph-XL extracts location entities ("ANAT-DP") from each sentence; a lookup table built from Chest ImaGenome gold-standard bounding boxes maps each location to a 2D Gaussian prior. Prompts are standardized to the "{location} {pathology}" format. The curated set has 6,480 samples spanning 27 anatomical locations and 8 pathologies.

Results

Our fine-tuned LDM achieves SOTA phrase grounding on both benchmarks, outperforming strong contrastive baselines (BioViL, BioViL-T) on most pathologies. We report per-class mean CNR and mIoU.
